# Freelancers and independent contractors are the v1 reader

Status: accepted

Redline v1 is built for freelancers and independent contractors reading agreements
sent to them by clients. The research ranked this segment strongest on evidence
(>50% hit by client non-payment, an average disputed amount over $6,000, a displaced
lawyer cost of ~$400 per review, and a competitor already selling flat-fee reviews at
$99 to exactly these people).

## Considered options

**Job-seekers reviewing employment agreements and non-competes** carry higher price
anchors ($500–$2,000, ~$1,000 for a non-compete review) and a sharper urgency moment —
the 72 hours between offer and signature. They were rejected because a job-seeker signs
roughly once every few years, and two of the six v1 capabilities — the saved document
library and the persistent red line list — only pay off with repeat use. Serving
job-seekers would mean cutting a third of the product.

**Renters, creators, and small landlords** were rejected on evidence: renters have a
strong cost anchor ($660 average lease review) but no data on willingness to pay;
creators self-select out of paid review below ~$10,000 deals; small landlords have no
segment-specific evidence at all.

## Consequences

- **We serve a minority of our own segment.** Only 28% of freelancers use a contract for any given gig. Redline reads documents, so the 72% who work without one — the group most exposed to the $6,000 dispute — get nothing from v1. This is a known, accepted hole, not an oversight.
- **Generating an agreement is deferred, not rejected.** It is the obvious way to reach the other 72%, and it is out of scope until the reading product finds fit. Revisit this ADR then rather than expanding scope quietly.
- The highest willingness-to-pay evidence in the research belongs to a segment we are not serving. Expect v1 revenue per document to be low.
