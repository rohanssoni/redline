## What to build
Every semantic red-line match is reviewed by a **second, independent model call**
— an LLM-as-judge — that decides whether the match reasonably fits the red line's
intent. Independent means its own call with its own prompt, not a field bolted
onto the analysis response.

When the judge disagrees, the disagreement is **logged for later audit only**.
The flag renders **exactly** as it would otherwise: not suppressed, not marked
down, no confidence marker, no reordering. Holding the flag back would break
ADR-0013's guarantee that a red line is never silently dropped, and showing a
marker would stretch ADR-0010's hedging bound to a kind of uncertainty the reader
has no way to check.

A judge call **failure** must not block or alter the flag either. The flag is the
guarantee; the judge is a signal about the matcher.

The judge's output is a review-priority signal, **not proof of correctness** — it
shares the blind spots of the model it checks. Label it that way wherever it is
reported. A human-labeled test set is explicitly out of scope.

Write the judge-disagreement log with the red line, the source sentence, and the
judge's reasoning, and make the agreement/disagreement rate queryable for audit.
The table and its policies go in a migration under `supabase/migrations/`.
Nothing about the judge is reachable from the reader-facing UI.

## Tests
- Each red-line-triggered flag gets exactly **one** judge call. Count them.
- The named test: **when the fake judge disagrees, the flag is still present in
  the output and identical to the agree case.** Compare the two outputs
  field-for-field, not just "it exists".
- A disagreement is written to the log with the red line, source sentence and
  reasoning.
- A judge call that **throws** does not block or alter the flag.
- Nothing in the reader-facing output carries the judge's verdict — assert the
  absence, since that is the ADR-0019 requirement.
